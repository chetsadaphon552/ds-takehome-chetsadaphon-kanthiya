PROBLEM 2.B1 - PCA AS A CONSTRAINED VARIANCE MAXIMIZATION

Setup
-----
Let X be a centered n-by-p data matrix and let

    S = X^T X / (n - 1)

be its sample covariance matrix. For a unit direction w in R^p, the projected
observations are z = Xw and their sample variance is:

    Var(Xw) = z^T z / (n - 1)
            = w^T X^T X w / (n - 1)
            = w^T S w

Optimization and Lagrange multiplier
-------------------------------------
We maximize w^T S w subject to w^T w = 1. Define:

    L(w, lambda) = w^T S w - lambda (w^T w - 1)

Because S is symmetric, differentiating with respect to w gives:

    grad_w L = 2 S w - 2 lambda w = 0
    S w = lambda w

Thus every stationary direction is an eigenvector of S. Multiplying the
eigenvalue equation by w^T and using w^T w = 1 gives:

    w^T S w = lambda

The variance is therefore the corresponding eigenvalue. The maximum is
attained by an eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of S. This
direction is the first principal component.

Further components
------------------
The second and subsequent principal directions are the eigenvectors associated
with the next-largest eigenvalues, subject to orthogonality to the previous
directions. The fraction of variance explained by the first k components is:

    (lambda_1 + ... + lambda_k) / (lambda_1 + ... + lambda_p)

The derivation, SVD relationship, and standardization example are also covered
in src/q2/1_ownership_pca.ipynb.
